⚡ In brief

Research. This month's studies circled one question: when does adding technology, intensity, or contact actually beat the simple version? Blended apps and stratified digital care often did not clearly beat good usual physiotherapy, yet real-time AI feedback for shoulder pain and wearable sensors after hip replacement did help. A stepped-wedge trial (RESOLVE) cut opioid prescriptions 30 percent without moving other outcomes, and a review put a number on enough back-pain walking, about 1,000 minutes over eight weeks. Standouts: the inka trial, where an intensive diet did not match knee replacement, and a 55-trial review finding little real benefit from radiofrequency denervation.

The market. The month's MSK news came from enterprise contracts and public-market signals rather than blockbuster funding. Physitrack locked in a five-year UK hospital deal and turned on its US remote therapeutic monitoring product, Hinge Health headed toward its first summer earnings as a profitable public company, and DarioHealth raised 23.5 million dollars for its multi-condition platform.

Policy. Washington dominated. 1
Conservative care & prevention
The RESOLVE Trial: A Stepped-Wedge, Cluster-Randomized Clinical Trial Evaluating the Effects of Clinical Practice Guidelines and Psychologically Informed Physical Therapy on Low Back Pain Outcomes in Service Members and Veterans.
Physical Therapy · Stepped-wedge cluster RCT · PMID 42472470 · DOI
Beisheim-Ryan EH et al.

Background. Guidelines say low back pain care should blend active exercise, hands-on treatment, and attention to the psychological side of pain, but it is unclear whether training whole clinics to deliver that package changes real-world outcomes.

What they did. Across six military and veteran clinics, 74 physical therapists were trained to deliver guideline-concordant, psychologically informed care and given monthly feedback, and disability, pain, and one-year health care use were compared before and after training among 2,320 patients in a stepped-wedge design.

What they found. Patients treated after the training improved slightly more on disability and pain but not by a clinically meaningful margin, and most practice patterns barely moved. The one clear win was a 30 percent drop in 12-month opioid prescriptions.

Bottom line. The authors conclude that training clinicians in the full guideline package is hard to convert into large outcome gains, though it may still curb opioid use.

2
Conservative care
Intensive diet or knee arthroplasty for severe knee osteoarthritis: The inka randomised trial.
Osteoarthritis and Cartilage · Non-inferiority RCT · PMID 42431586 · DOI
Henriksen M et al.

Background. For severe knee osteoarthritis, patients and surgeons want to know whether an intensive weight-loss program could work as well as a knee replacement in people heavy enough to qualify for surgery.

What they did. This non-inferiority trial randomly assigned 92 adults with obesity and severe knee osteoarthritis who were eligible for knee replacement to either a 12-week low-calorie meal-replacement diet with group coaching or to knee replacement surgery, measuring knee pain and function at six months.

What they found. Surgery clearly won on knee symptoms (Oxford Knee Score 36.5 versus 28.2), so the diet was not non-inferior, but the diet group still lost about 11 kilograms (10 percent of body weight) with similar adverse events. Recruitment fell short of target.

Bottom line. The authors conclude that for people already eligible for surgery, an intensive diet does not match knee replacement for symptom relief, even though it delivers real weight loss.

3
Conservative care
How much walking is enough for chronic low back pain? A systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis.
PM&R · Dose-response meta-analysis · PMID 42479922 · DOI
Nunez-Cortes R et al.

Background. Walking is the simplest and cheapest exercise for chronic low back pain, but how much walking is enough has never been pinned down.

What they did. This review pooled 13 randomized trials of walking-only programs for chronic low back pain and used a dose-response model to find the amount of walking linked to the biggest improvements in pain and disability.

What they found. Benefit followed a U-shaped curve, with the largest pain reduction near 1,000 minutes of walking accrued over roughly eight weeks (about 125 minutes a week) and the largest disability reduction near 900 minutes; outdoor walking and motivational support trended better.

Bottom line. The authors suggest a concrete starting target of roughly 1,000 minutes of walking over eight weeks for people with chronic low back pain.

4
Digital & hybrid delivery
Effectiveness of an individualised swimming and education programme for chronic low back pain (EduSwim): a randomised controlled trial.
British Journal of Sports Medicine · RCT · PMID 42481208 · DOI
Wareham DM et al.

Background. Swimming is often suggested for back pain, but it has rarely been tested, and delivering it through telehealth coaching is untried.

What they did. This trial randomly assigned 76 adults with chronic low back pain to an individualized swimming-plus-education program guided by four physiotherapy telehealth sessions, or to education alone with up to two telehealth sessions, measuring disability at eight weeks and again at one year.

What they found. Swimming plus education beat education alone for disability at eight weeks (about 2.5 points on a 24-point scale), but the edge faded by 52 weeks, pain differences were small, and the swimming group had slightly more, mostly minor, adverse events.

Bottom line. The authors conclude swimming with education helps in the short term, a telehealth-coached option worth having, though the benefit does not clearly last.

5
Digital & hybrid delivery
Comparative Effectiveness of AI-Assisted Telerehabilitation, Telerehabilitation, In-Person Care, and Usual Care for Chronic Nonspecific Low Back Pain: Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis.
Journal of Medical Internet Research · Network meta-analysis · PMID 42398934 · DOI
Gu P et al.

Background. Guided exercise is the core of back-pain rehab, and telerehabilitation can deliver it remotely, but how remote and AI-assisted options compare to in-person care is unclear.

What they did. This Bayesian network meta-analysis pooled 20 randomized trials (1,854 patients) with chronic nonspecific low back pain to rank telerehabilitation, in-person rehabilitation, usual care, and AI-assisted telerehabilitation for pain, disability, and fear of movement.

What they found. Telerehabilitation performed comparably to in-person care and better than usual care, and AI-assisted telerehabilitation ranked highly as an emerging option, though the evidence behind the AI arm was thin and uncertain.

Bottom line. The authors conclude remote delivery is a credible alternative to in-person rehab, with AI-assisted versions promising but not yet proven.

Health economics
Cost-effectiveness of stratified physiotherapy integrated with digital health versus usual physiotherapy for neck and/or shoulder pain: results of a cluster randomised clinical trial.
Archives of Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation · Economic evaluation alongside RCT · PMID 42501929 · DOI
van Tilburg ML et al.

Background. Blending physiotherapy with an app and matching intensity to a patient's risk sounds efficient, but whether it saves money or improves quality of life is untested for neck and shoulder pain.

What they did. Alongside a cluster-randomized trial in 24 Dutch practices, researchers compared a stratified, digitally blended physiotherapy approach against usual physiotherapy for 136 patients with neck or shoulder pain, tracking quality-adjusted life years and costs over nine months.

What they found. The blended approach produced essentially no difference in quality of life and only tiny, uncertain cost differences; it looked slightly more favorable from a health-system view but not from a societal one.

Bottom line. The authors conclude that risk stratification plus blended digital care did not clearly improve value over usual physiotherapy, and other strategies are worth exploring.

7
Digital & hybrid delivery
Effectiveness of an AI-based home exercise app for rehabilitation of rotator cuff-related shoulder pain: A randomized controlled trial.
Musculoskeletal Science & Practice · RCT · PMID 42531919 · DOI
Villar-Alises O et al.

Background. Exercise is first-line for rotator cuff shoulder pain, but weak supervision and adherence blunt its effect, and real-time digital feedback might help.

What they did. This trial randomly assigned 46 adults with rotator cuff-related shoulder pain to standard physiotherapy or physiotherapy plus an AI app that used a phone camera to give real-time form feedback over 12 weeks.

What they found. The app group had greater pain relief that passed the meaningful threshold at 4 and 12 weeks, better early function, lower fear-avoidance beliefs, and fewer patients needing further care (3 versus 10).

Bottom line. The authors conclude that adding AI-guided home exercise improved pain and cut downstream health care use for shoulder pain.

8
Digital & hybrid delivery
The Efficiency and Cost-Effectiveness of Wearable Sensors in a Digital Physiotherapeutic Total Hip Arthroplasty-Specific Training System for Patients After Total Hip Arthroplasty: Randomized Controlled Trial.
JMIR mHealth and uHealth · RCT · PMID 42479868 · DOI
Jiang C et al.

Background. With hip replacements rising, home rehab needs to scale, and motion sensors that give feedback might improve how well patients do their exercises.

What they did. This Shanghai trial randomly assigned 240 patients after hip replacement to a 12-week app-based home exercise program either with or without wearable motion sensors for real-time feedback, comparing hip function and cost-effectiveness at 24 weeks.

What they found. The sensor group improved significantly more on the primary hip score at 24 weeks, with especially large early gains at six weeks across most measures.

Bottom line. The authors conclude that adding wearable sensor feedback to app-based home rehab meaningfully improves recovery after hip replacement.

9
Digital & hybrid delivery
Effects of different breathing patterns combined with core stability exercises in a hybrid telerehabilitation program for chronic nonspecific low back pain: a randomized controlled trial.
Disability and Rehabilitation · RCT · PMID 42445949 · DOI
Zou H et al.

Background. Core exercises help back pain, and adding breathing training might do more, especially when delivered partly online.

What they did. This assessor-blinded trial assigned 54 adults with chronic nonspecific low back pain to core stability exercise alone or combined with one of two breathing techniques, all in a four-week hybrid telerehabilitation program with three-month follow-up.

What they found. Both breathing-plus-core groups reduced pain and disability more than core exercise alone, with added gains in fear of movement and balance and partial persistence at three months; the two breathing styles performed similarly.

Bottom line. The authors conclude breathing training adds value to core exercise for back pain in a hybrid model, though the trial was small.

10
Conservative care & evidence
Current evidence on radiofrequency denervation for chronic low back pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis.
BMJ Open · Systematic review & meta-analysis · PMID 42463214 · DOI
Truong KMTL et al.

Background. Radiofrequency denervation, which burns nerves thought to carry back pain signals, is widely used, but its true benefit is contested.

What they did. This review pooled 55 randomized trials (4,394 patients) testing radiofrequency denervation against a sham procedure for facet-joint, sacroiliac, discogenic, and nonspecific chronic low back pain.

What they found. The procedure produced only small improvements in pain and function versus sham, with low-to-moderate certainty, and none reached the threshold for a clinically important difference.

Bottom line. The authors conclude the evidence does not support meaningful benefit from radiofrequency denervation, a caution for a common and costly procedure.

11
Interventional & devices
Restorative Neurostimulation Outcomes for Mechanical Chronic Low Back Pain: Delayed-Start Crossover from Optimal Medical Management and 2-Year Treatment Longitudinal Analyses from the RESTORE Randomized Controlled Trial.
The Spine Journal · RCT crossover extension · PMID 42462977 · DOI
Schwab F et al.

Background. A subset of chronic low back pain comes from poor control of a deep spinal muscle, and an implanted device that stimulates that muscle aims to fix the underlying problem.

What they did. This extension of a 203-patient randomized trial followed the original device group for two years and let control patients cross over to the implant after the randomized phase, tracking disability, pain, and quality of life.

What they found. Device-group improvements held at two years (about 25-point disability and 4-point pain drops), and most control patients chose to cross over and then improved after a year of stimulation.

Bottom line. The authors report durable benefit from restorative neurostimulation, though the crossover comparison is not a randomized one.

12
Patient education
Pain neuroscience education versus spinal physiology and ergonomics education in multidisciplinary rehabilitation for chronic low back pain: 12-month follow-up of a randomized controlled trial.
Physiotherapy Theory and Practice · RCT · PMID 42482622 · DOI
Adenis N et al.

Background. Teaching patients how pain works, called pain neuroscience education, is popular, but whether it beats plain back-and-ergonomics teaching over the long run is unclear.

What they did. This trial's 12-month follow-up compared pain neuroscience education against spinal physiology and ergonomics education, both delivered inside a one-week multidisciplinary program, in 74 adults with chronic low back pain.

What they found. At one year the two approaches produced similar disability and pain, but pain neuroscience education left patients with less fear of movement.

Bottom line. The authors conclude pain neuroscience education mainly helps by reducing fear of movement, not by improving disability over standard education.

13
Scalable delivery
An Internet-based Cognitive Behavioral Program, With Telephone Support, Improves Pain, Disability and Emotional Wellbeing in Adults with Chronic Pain: Randomized Controlled Trial of the Pain Course With 2-year Follow-up.
Clinical Journal of Pain · RCT · PMID 42528312 · DOI
Navin C et al.

Background. Online CBT could widen access to psychological pain care, but questions remain about whether the benefits last.

What they did. This Irish trial randomly assigned 133 adults with chronic pain to a clinician-supported online CBT program (the Pain Course, five lessons over eight weeks) or delayed access, following outcomes out to two years.

What they found. The program improved disability, mood, and pain with moderate-to-large effects that held at three months, and among the minority who stayed in the study, gains largely persisted at two years, with average clinician time under 90 minutes per patient.

Bottom line. The authors conclude clinician-supported online CBT is an efficient option with durable benefit for those who respond.

14
Conservative care
Do blood flow restriction exercises offer additional benefits when compared to conventional exercises in musculoskeletal rehabilitation? A systematic review and meta-analysis.
British Journal of Sports Medicine · Systematic review & meta-analysis · PMID 42399091 · DOI
Ferlito JV et al.

Background. Blood flow restriction lets people build strength with light loads by partly limiting blood flow to a limb, an appealing option when heavy lifting is not tolerated.

What they did. This review pooled 45 randomized trials (1,652 participants) comparing light-load exercise with blood flow restriction against light-load and heavy-load exercise in musculoskeletal conditions.

What they found. Blood flow restriction beat light-load exercise for strength and disability but matched, not exceeded, heavy-load exercise, and pain differences were small; certainty was low and adverse-event reporting inconsistent.

Bottom line. The authors conclude blood flow restriction is a useful substitute when heavy loading is not an option, but it does not outperform it.

15
Measurement
Convergent validity and responsiveness of the 9-item PROMIS-29 derived low back pain impact score.
The Journal of Pain · Measurement study (RCT data) · PMID 42471169 · DOI
Ebrahimi Z et al.

Background. A short nine-item back-pain impact score was proposed by an NIH task force, but its measurement quality had not been well tested.

What they did. Using data from 372 people with chronic low back pain in the WELBack trial, researchers checked how well the impact score agreed with established measures and detected real change over two months.

What they found. The score showed strong agreement with legacy questionnaires and acceptable ability to detect change, with a minimal important difference estimated at six points.

Bottom line. The authors conclude this brief score is a psychometrically sound, practical way to capture back-pain impact in clinics and research.

16
Self-management & adherence
Adherence to exercise intervention including pain threshold, exercise and physiotherapy sessions for patients with chronic rotator cuff tendinopathy.
Archives of Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation · Secondary analysis of RCT · PMID 42457108 · DOI
Axman S et al.

Background. Exercise programs for shoulder tendon pain often ask patients to work within a set pain limit, but whether people actually stick to those limits and their logs is rarely checked.

What they did. This secondary analysis of an 84-patient shoulder tendinopathy trial tracked how well patients kept to their assigned pain limits (pain allowed up to 5 versus kept below 3) and how faithfully they logged exercise and attended physiotherapy over 26 weeks.

What they found. Patients did keep to their assigned pain levels and attended most sessions, but diary logging slipped over time, especially after supervised physiotherapy ended.

Bottom line. The authors conclude patients can follow pain-guided exercise rules, but self-logging fades once clinician support stops, a caution for unsupervised programs.

17
Health economics & surgical
Cost-Effectiveness of Biportal Endoscopic Spine Surgery Compared with Microscopic Surgery for Lumbar Degenerative Diseases: A Pooled Analysis of Two Randomized Controlled Trials.
The Spine Journal · Pooled economic RCT analysis · PMID 42480657 · DOI
Park SM et al.

Background. A newer keyhole spine surgery technique shows clinical promise, but its value for money against standard microscopic surgery is unclear.

What they did. Pooling two randomized trials of 187 patients in South Korea, researchers compared the cost per quality-of-life year gained for biportal endoscopic spine surgery versus microscopic surgery for lumbar disc and stenosis problems.

What they found. The two surgeries produced similar quality-of-life gains at similar cost, and the endoscopic option had a roughly 95 percent probability of being cost-effective at the local willingness-to-pay threshold.

Bottom line. The authors conclude the endoscopic technique is a cost-effective alternative to standard microscopic spine surgery.

18
Care models & workforce
Digitally delivered education for healthcare professionals managing persistent musculoskeletal pain: a scoping review.
Musculoskeletal Science & Practice · Scoping review · PMID 42485866 · DOI
Coyne L et al.

Background. Online courses are increasingly used to train clinicians who treat persistent musculoskeletal pain, but it is unclear how well they are designed or whether they change practice.

What they did. This scoping review charted 23 studies of fully digital pain-education programs for clinicians, mostly physiotherapists, rating how completely each was described and what level of outcome it measured.

What they found. Programs varied widely and were often incompletely reported, with little tailoring or interactivity; clinicians liked them, learning results were mixed, and almost none measured whether patient care actually improved.

Bottom line. The authors conclude digital clinician education is popular but under-evaluated, rarely reaching the question of whether patients benefit.

19
Patient education
Immersive learning: The role of virtual reality in patient education for musculoskeletal conditions - A systematic review and meta-analysis.
Physiotherapy · Systematic review & meta-analysis · PMID 42385546 · DOI
Jahromi MM et al.

Background. Virtual reality can make patient education more immersive, but whether that helps musculoskeletal pain is uncertain.

What they did. This review pooled 21 studies (18 randomized) of virtual-reality-delivered patient education for musculoskeletal conditions, most in low back pain, looking mainly at pain.

What they found. Virtual reality education produced a small-to-moderate reduction in back pain across seven trials, plus gains in function and psychological measures, and was well tolerated aside from occasional mild dizziness.

Bottom line. The authors conclude virtual reality is a promising, engaging way to deliver patient education for musculoskeletal pain.

Care models & workforce
Cluster randomised feasibility trial of PRISM: the PRimary Care Individual Social Norms MSK Data Dashboard to support first contact physiotherapy management of musculoskeletal patients in primary care.
BMJ Open · Feasibility RCT protocol · PMID 42481200 · DOI
Dunphy E et al.

Background. First-contact physiotherapists now handle many musculoskeletal patients in primary care, but working alone they vary widely in decisions, which can affect quality.

What they did. This is the plan for a feasibility trial of PRISM, a dashboard that shows each clinician how their referral and treatment choices compare to peers (for example, you refer to surgery more than 90 percent of colleagues), tested across four UK primary-care sites against usual care.

What they found. No results yet; the study will mainly test whether sites, clinicians, and patients can be recruited and whether the dashboard is used.

Bottom line. Worth watching as an attempt to reduce unwarranted variation in front-line musculoskeletal care using peer-comparison nudges.

21
Access & equity
The Impact of Telehealth Use Experience on Recruitment of Underserved Populations Into a Telehealth-Delivered Mindfulness-Based Trial for Patients With Chronic Low Back Pain: Cross-Sectional Survey.
JMIR mHealth and uHealth · Cross-sectional survey · PMID 42525540 · DOI
Foster MV et al.

Background. Telehealth could widen access to back-pain care for underserved patients, but it is unclear who already has the experience and comfort to use it.

What they did. Using baseline data from 244 mostly Black and safety-net patients enrolling in a telehealth mindfulness trial for chronic low back pain, researchers examined which characteristics predicted prior telehealth use.

What they found. Most (71 percent) had used telehealth before; lower use was linked to Black or Hispanic identity, part-time work, and low comfort with video, while more education and longer pain duration went with more experience, yet these gaps did not stop people from enrolling.

Bottom line. The authors conclude that outreach to build digital comfort could make telehealth back-pain programs more inclusive.

22
Access & equity
Sociodemographic Factors, but Not Rurality, Explain Limited Variance in Fibromyalgia Severity: A Secondary Analysis of Two Randomized Clinical Trials.
Pain Medicine · Secondary analysis of RCTs · PMID 42434958 · DOI
Gillette EY et al.

Background. It is often assumed rural patients fare worse with fibromyalgia, but the evidence is thin.

What they did. This secondary analysis combined baseline data from 685 people in two fibromyalgia trials to test whether living rurally, or other sociodemographic factors, explained differences in symptom severity.

What they found. Rurality had no meaningful link to severity, and sociodemographic factors together explained less than 10 percent of it, though older, non-White, unemployed, and lower-income patients tended to be more severe.

Bottom line. The authors conclude fibromyalgia severity is only weakly tied to who or where patients are, pushing attention back to clinical drivers.

23
Care models
TaILOR: a randomised trial to compare the clinical and cost-effectiveness of a patient-initiated follow-up (PIFU) strategy compared to standard care pathways in people with inflammatory arthritis: a study protocol.
Trials · RCT protocol · PMID 42400057 · DOI
Brewer G et al.

Background. Health systems are shifting to patient-initiated follow-up, where patients book appointments only when they need them, but this is untested in most inflammatory arthritis.

What they did. This is the plan for a 438-patient UK trial across 32 sites comparing patient-initiated follow-up (with a safety-net appointment at 24 months) against standard scheduled visits for people with stable inflammatory arthritis, with quality of life at two years the main measure.

What they found. No results yet; the trial will also weigh cost-effectiveness, flares, and patient confidence.

Bottom line. Worth watching as a test of whether on-demand follow-up is safe and efficient for long-term musculoskeletal conditions.

24
Wearables & measurement
Sensor-based measures of knee brace adherence have low agreement with self-report methods: A multi-measure study among knee osteoarthritis patients.
Osteoarthritis and Cartilage · Sensor sub-study within an RCT · PMID 42442456 · DOI
Callaghan MJ et al.

Background. Knee braces only help osteoarthritis if patients actually wear them, yet trials usually judge that from patients reporting their own use.

What they did. Nested inside a knee osteoarthritis brace trial, researchers compared three ways of tracking wear over 26 weeks in 60 patients: questionnaires, weekly text-message check-ins, and a hidden temperature sensor built into the brace.

What they found. Self-report painted a rosier picture than the sensor: about 83 percent looked adherent by questionnaire versus 60 percent by sensor at 12 weeks, and 72 versus 43 percent by 26 weeks, with most of the drop-off in the first three months.

Bottom line. The authors conclude self-report substantially overstates device use, a real caution for any program that judges, or bills for, adherence without objective sensor data.

Interventional & devices
Long-term benefits of ultrasound-guided percutaneous electrical nerve stimulation for chronic lateral elbow pain: a randomized controlled trial.
Archives of Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation · Double-blind sham-controlled RCT · PMID 42457107 · DOI
Albert-Lucena D et al.

Background. Chronic tennis elbow is stubborn, and it is unclear whether adding targeted electrical nerve stimulation to exercise beats a convincing placebo.

What they did. This double-blind trial randomized 49 adults with chronic lateral elbow pain to a four-week exercise program plus weekly ultrasound-guided percutaneous electrical stimulation of the radial nerve, or the same with a sham version, following them for a year.

What they found. Real stimulation beat sham across pain, disability, fear of movement, and catastrophizing, with improvements that passed clinical-importance thresholds and held at one year; more than 90 percent reported meaningful improvement and there were no serious adverse events.

Bottom line. The authors conclude that adding ultrasound-guided nerve stimulation to exercise produced durable, clinically meaningful gains for tennis elbow, a strong result for a hard-to-treat condition.

📈 The market

Health tech and health-system innovation: the companies, products, and delivery models reshaping how MSK care gets built and bought.

Physitrack locked in a five-year UK hospital deal.
Physitrack, a listed digital physiotherapy and remote-monitoring platform, signed a five-year enterprise agreement with a UK private hospital network of more than 30 hospitals, deploying over 400 clinician licenses (worth about 255,000 pounds in total). The signal for MSK builders is that health systems increasingly want one standardized digital rehab layer across every site rather than clinic-by-clinic tools, and incumbents win these by converting existing customers to the full platform. Yahoo Finance
Physitrack switched on remote therapeutic monitoring in the US.
In its Q2 report, Physitrack said its remote therapeutic monitoring product is now live in the US and its digital-therapy segment is accelerating. Remote therapeutic monitoring is the US pathway that pays clinicians to monitor patients doing home exercise between visits, so a European vendor going live with it shows the US MSK monitoring market is now the growth prize everyone is chasing. interim report
Hinge Health headed into its first public-company summer.
Hinge Health set August 4 as its first summer earnings date as a public company, and days later an early backer, Insight Holdings, sold about 38 million dollars of stock after the shares roughly doubled over a year. This is the first full quarter of scrutiny on whether the largest digital MSK company can keep growing in public markets, and insider selling into strength is the kind of signal partners and competitors watch when judging the category's staying power. earnings date · insider sale
DarioHealth raised 23.5 million dollars for its multi-condition platform.
DarioHealth, a chronic-condition digital platform that includes a musculoskeletal program, priced a 23.5 million dollar registered direct offering to institutional investors. A publicly traded, cash-tight player raising growth capital shows continued investor appetite for platforms that bundle MSK alongside cardiometabolic and behavioral care, the one-platform-many-conditions model that Sword and Hinge are also chasing. announcement
Value-based MSK crossed nearly a million lives.
HOPCo, an integrated musculoskeletal value-based-care company, brought more than 845,000 new members onto its population-health platform across five states, a go-live timed for July after being announced in early June. It is a reminder that the biggest MSK growth is in payer and population contracts, not just employer apps, and that getting paid to keep a whole population's joint and back care appropriate is now scaling to nearly a million people at once. PR Newswire
Digital-health funding rebounded, and MSK's Hinge became the proof point.
Rock Health's mid-year report found US digital health startups raised 7.4 billion dollars in the first half of 2026, about a billion above last year, with capital concentrating in a few large, AI-driven winners. It singled out Hinge Health, with 182 million dollars in quarterly revenue up 47 percent and positive cash flow, as the sector's evidence that digital MSK can be a real business rather than a pilot. 🏛️ Policy

Rules, regulation, and reimbursement across Medicare, Medicaid, and commercial payers: how the MSK delivery and payment ecosystem is evolving.

CMS proposed the 2027 fee schedule: a lower base rate, but a likely raise for PT.
On July 14 CMS released its proposed 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule. The conversion factor, the dollar multiplier that turns each service into a payment, would fall about 1.7 percent for non-alternative-payment clinicians (to roughly 32.84 dollars) as a one-year bonus expires. Even so, CMS estimates physical therapy could see a 1 to 3 percent overall increase because of proposed changes to how overhead costs are calculated, and the documentation threshold that triggers extra paperwork rises with inflation. Comments are open through mid-September. CMS fact sheet · APTA
The same rule would tighten remote therapeutic monitoring.
Remote therapeutic monitoring lets physical therapists bill Medicare (CPT codes 98975 through 98981) for setting a patient up with a connected device or app and reviewing the data between visits. The proposed 2027 rule would limit it to established patients, require a separate initiating visit, allow billing only when the practice's own clinical staff do the work, and lower the payment because devices now cost less. CMS is also floating a plan to bundle these codes into new ones. For hybrid and tech-enabled PT models, this trims a growing revenue lever. CMS fact sheet
Medicare's AI prior-authorization pilot drew a congressional backlash.
The WISeR model, which for the first time applies prior authorization to traditional Medicare for procedures prone to overuse, targets a list that reads like a spine and pain roster: epidural steroid injections, nerve-stimulator implants, vertebral augmentation, and knee arthroscopy for osteoarthritis. In July the Senate narrowly rejected a repeal (46 to 50), a House committee moved to block its funding, and Democrats pressed CMS for denial data. Critics object that contractors are paid a share of the spending they prevent; CMS says a gold-carding exemption will spare clinicians with high approval rates. Becker's
Congress advanced Medicare Advantage prior-authorization reform.
Two House committees unanimously passed the Improving Seniors' Timely Access to Care Act, which would force private Medicare plans to answer prior-authorization requests within seven days (72 hours when urgent), move to real-time electronic review, and publish denial data. Many MSK patients, from joint-replacement rehab to spine care, sit in these plans, so faster decisions would ease access to therapy, though an amendment pushed the effective date to 2029 and it would not touch the traditional-Medicare WISeR pilot. Becker's
Therapist telehealth is locked in through 2027, with a mandatory back pain model on deck.
Two structural certainties frame the year. Medicare's rules letting physical, occupational, and speech therapists bill telehealth from a patient's home run through December 31, 2027, giving hybrid models a stable planning runway. And the Ambulatory Specialty Model, a mandatory program that puts low back pain specialists on two-sided risk with payment swings up to 9 percent, starts in January 2027. A required model built around low back pain rewards exactly the function and avoided-utilization outcomes that conservative and digital MSK care are designed to produce. AOTA · CMS ASM
