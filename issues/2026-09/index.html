🏛️ Policy

Rules, regulation, and reimbursement across Medicare, Medicaid, and commercial payers: how the MSK delivery and payment ecosystem is evolving.

More than 230 organizations asked CMS to drop its remote monitoring restrictions.
Comments on the CY 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule proposed rule closed September 14. A coalition letter organized by the Alliance for Connected Care, signed by more than 230 organizations, urged CMS not to finalize proposals that would pay for RPM and RTM only when clinical staff are direct employees of the billing practice, require an initiating visit before monitoring, and limit RTM to established patients. The coalition estimates the changes could disrupt care for more than 1 million beneficiaries. Epstein Becker Green, filing for remote monitoring practices, reported that posted comments on the rule exceeded 44,000. No final rule has been issued. RTM is the main Medicare billing path for hybrid PT, and contracted monitoring staff are common in PT practices. Telehealth.org · Epstein Becker Green
APTA modeled a multiyear PT payment gain in the fee schedule proposal.
On September 1, APTA published staff modeling of CMS data released after the proposed rule. CMS proposes to allocate indirect practice costs to individual services rather than to specialties as a whole, a method change that favors professions with low overhead such as physical therapy. APTA estimates practice expense RVUs for the most commonly used PT codes would rise 35 to 45 percent over eight years, roughly a 1 to 3 percent payment increase per year, and expects other specialties to oppose the change. These are APTA estimates, not CMS figures. If finalized, it would be the first sustained structural gain for PT payment in years and would partly offset conversion factor cuts. APTA News
UnitedHealthcare's prior authorization cuts arrived without PT codes.
UnitedHealthcare posted its code lists on September 1 for the 30 percent reduction in prior authorization taking effect October 1, removing about 1,700 codes across commercial, Medicare Advantage, Community, Exchange, and Oxford plans. Announcements listed physical, occupational, and speech therapy among affected areas, and last month's issue repeated that framing. On September 25, APTA reported that none of the codes physical therapists commonly use are on the lists and said it had contacted UHC. A review of the commercial and Medicare Advantage lists for this issue found no 97000-series therapy codes; the lists do include some MSK injections, orthopedic procedures, and, for Medicare Advantage, chiropractic manipulation codes. PT clinics should keep checking UHC's authorization lookup tool. UHC provider notice · APTA · Healthcare Finance News
A stopgap spending bill averted a shutdown, and therapist telehealth is safe through 2027.
The President signed H.R. 6500, the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, on September 2, funding federal agencies through December 11, 2026. The September 30 date mattered for funding only. Medicare telehealth flexibilities, including the authority for physical therapists, occupational therapists, and speech-language pathologists to bill telehealth, already run through December 31, 2027 under the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026. Permanent legislation such as the Expanded Telehealth Access Act remains in committee. Medicare PT telehealth billing is stable for another 15 months, and the next funding deadline to watch is December 11. The White House · Epstein Becker Green
CMS published the final participant list for the mandatory low back pain model.
On September 15, CMS posted the final participant list for the first performance year of the Ambulatory Specialty Model as a public dataset. The model is mandatory, selects individual physicians, and covers heart failure and low back pain across five performance years from January 1, 2027 through December 31, 2031. The low back pain cohort draws from specialties such as orthopedic surgery, neurosurgery, pain management, and physical medicine and rehabilitation; physical therapists are not eligible. Selected spine and pain physicians will be measured on low back pain episode cost and quality starting in January, which gives them a reason to refer to physical therapy earlier and gives PT practices a reason to find out who is on the list. A day earlier, Sword announced a technology cooperation agreement with Brazil's Ministry of Health covering AI for triage, waiting-list prioritization, and clinical decision support across the public system, with technical work starting in early 2027. Purchasers are putting digital health vendors on performance contracts.
The Peterson Health Technology Institute released its 2026 State of Digital Health Purchasing survey on September 29, covering 321 decision-makers at health plans, employers, and health systems. Performance-based contracts are in use at 68 percent of employers and 55 percent of health plans, and in 85 percent of those contracts at least a quarter of fees are at risk. Purchasers struggle to check vendor claims: 45 percent find vendor data hard to combine with their own claims, and 38 percent cannot independently verify reported outcomes. Nearly half say fewer than a quarter of eligible members enroll. For digital MSK vendors, outcomes guarantees are now standard terms, and verifiable data is the competitive edge. PHTI announcement

🔬 The research

A note on this month's set: several studies asked what technology and new care models add on top of good basics. Robots, apps, live video, and educational films were each compared with simpler care, and the evidence of added benefit was often modest.

Each study links to its PubMed record and DOI.

1
Conservative care
Optimal exercise dose in adults with chronic low back pain disorders: systematic review with dose-response network meta-analysis.
British Journal of Sports Medicine · Dose-response network meta-analysis · PMID 42785965 · DOI
Arora NK et al.

Background. Exercise is the default prescription for chronic low back pain, and clinicians still guess at how many sessions, how long, and for how many weeks.

What they did. This network meta-analysis pooled 239 randomized trials of exercise for chronic low back pain and modeled how session frequency, session length, and program duration related to disability and pain.

What they found. The model-predicted best dose for short-term disability was four sessions a week of 25 minutes for 13 weeks, and for short-term pain three sessions a week of 30 minutes for 8 weeks. Higher doses showed little added benefit, credible intervals overlapped for most dose levels, few doses stayed clinically meaningful past three months, and 84 percent of trials were at high risk of bias.

Bottom line. Low to moderate doses capture most of the short-term benefit. The evidence does not establish one optimal dose, so patient preference and adherence should still drive the plan.

2
Conservative care
Exercise Therapy and Education for Young Adults With Persistent Knee Problems After Anterior Cruciate Ligament Reconstruction: A Randomized Clinical Trial.
Annals of Internal Medicine · Randomized clinical trial · PMID 42804769 · DOI
Culvenor AG et al.

Background. Many young adults still have knee pain and early signs of osteoarthritis years after ACL reconstruction, and there is little trial evidence on what to do for them.

What they did. The SUPER trial randomized 184 adults aged 18 to 40, on average two years after reconstruction, to four months of twice-weekly physiotherapist-supervised exercise with education, or to a single education session plus a self-directed exercise resource.

What they found. At four months the supervised group improved 4.8 points more on the KOOS4 knee score (95 percent CI 1.4 to 8.2), gained more strength, and more often rated their pain as improved (73 versus 40 percent). By 12 months the gap narrowed because the control group kept improving. Cartilage on MRI did not differ.

Bottom line. The trial provided evidence that supervised exercise speeds early recovery for persistent knee problems after ACL reconstruction, while the gap narrowed over a year as the self-directed group kept improving. That trade-off matters for how payers and clinics size these programs.

3
Conservative care
CFT+ With a Subsequent Interdisciplinary Pain Management Program (IPMP) If Needed Versus IPMP Alone for Patients With Severe Disabling Chronic LBP: Pragmatic Patient-Blinded RCT With Economic Analysis.
Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy · Pragmatic patient-blinded RCT with economic analysis · PMID 42676088 · DOI
Vaegter HB et al.

Background. Patients with severely disabling chronic low back pain are referred to interdisciplinary pain centers, and it was unclear whether starting care with cognitive functional therapy plus psychologist support (CFT+) could match the center's interdisciplinary pain management program (IPMP).

What they did. This pragmatic, patient-blinded trial randomized 133 adults with severely disabling chronic low back pain to three months of CFT+, with optional access to the interdisciplinary program afterward, or to the interdisciplinary program alone. The main outcome was an improvement of at least 8 points on the Oswestry Disability Index at 12 months, and the team also estimated cost-effectiveness.

What they found. At 12 months, 22 percent of the CFT+ group and 18 percent of the interdisciplinary program group reached that improvement (difference 3.9 percentage points, 95 percent CI -12.4 to 20.3). The study did not show a difference in average disability change or in the secondary outcomes, including pain and pain catastrophizing. Disability data were available for about 70 percent of the CFT+ group and 67 percent of the comparison group. The cost-effectiveness estimate for CFT+ versus the interdisciplinary program was about 53,000 euros per quality-adjusted life year.

Bottom line. The trial failed to show that CFT+ was superior to the interdisciplinary program for people with severely disabling back pain, and the wide confidence interval leaves room for a benefit or a harm. Whether it is a reasonable first step on cost or access grounds is not settled by this trial.

4
Interventional care
No Efficacy of Dexamethasone Injections for Acute Sciatica: A Double-Blind Randomized Study Versus Saline.
European Journal of Pain · Double-blind placebo-controlled RCT · PMID 42690210 · DOI
Maugars Y et al.

Background. Epidural steroid injections are used worldwide for sciatica, and the trials behind them are mostly of limited quality.

What they did. The DEXHIA trial randomized 106 adults with sciatica from a disc herniation of less than three months to an ultrasound-guided caudal injection of dexamethasone or of saline alone, measuring disability at three weeks.

What they found. Disability improved in both groups, and the study did not show a meaningful difference between them (Oswestry change of 9.5 points with dexamethasone and 13.8 with saline). The study found no difference in secondary outcomes at three weeks, three months, or six months, and rates of repeat injection and surgery were similar, and mild injection side effects were more common with dexamethasone.

Bottom line. For acute disc-related sciatica, this trial failed to show a benefit of a caudal dexamethasone injection over saline. That is one more reason to lead with time, education, and active care.

Digital & hybrid delivery
Effectiveness, Mediators and Moderators of Remote Live vs. Pre-Recorded Physical Exercise Training for Chronic Low Back Pain.
European Journal of Pain · Randomized controlled trial · PMID 42806217 · DOI
Gevers-Montoro C et al.

Background. Remote exercise programs are cheaper to scale when the sessions are pre-recorded, and it is unclear whether live video coaching earns its extra cost.

What they did. This trial randomized 94 adults with chronic low back pain to a waitlist or to 12 weeks of three-times-weekly exercise led by a kinesiologist, delivered either live over videoconference or as individual pre-recorded videos.

What they found. Both remote formats reduced pain intensity by more than 20 percent and reduced movement-evoked pain compared with waitlist, and the study did not show differences between live and pre-recorded delivery on pain, effort, fatigue, or satisfaction. Gains ran through more physical activity, faster walking speed, and better self-rated health. Only 57 of 94 participants completed the study.

Bottom line. This small trial found no evidence of a difference between pre-recorded and live remote exercise. If that holds, builders can scale recorded content and spend clinician time where it adds the most.

6
Digital & hybrid delivery
Digital therapeutic combining exercise and cognitive-behavioural therapy reduces pain in patients with chronic patellofemoral pain: a multicentre randomised controlled trial.
British Journal of Sports Medicine · Multicenter randomized controlled trial · PMID 42692805 · DOI
Park JY et al.

Background. Patellofemoral pain is common and often persistent, and exercise programs tend to fail on adherence once patients leave the clinic.

What they did. This trial at 10 Korean hospitals randomized 216 people with chronic patellofemoral pain to an eight-week smartphone program combining personalized home exercise, weekly cognitive behavioral sessions, and daily reflection worksheets, or to education with one-time exercise instruction and a paper diary.

What they found. At eight weeks the app group reported 12.5 mm less usual pain on a 100 mm scale (95 percent CI 7.8 to 17.1) and better knee function, quality of life, and catastrophizing scores. Exercise adherence topped 80 percent with no serious adverse events.

Bottom line. This trial provided evidence that packaging exercise with cognitive behavioral support in an app reduces pain more than a printed handout for patellofemoral pain. Longer follow-up and a comparison against in-person physical therapy are the next tests.

7
Digital & hybrid delivery
Extended Reality Interventions for Osteoarthritis of the Knee and Recovery After Total Knee Arthroplasty: Systematic Review and Meta-Analyses.
Journal of Medical Internet Research · Systematic review and meta-analyses · PMID 42714169 · DOI
Kalinowski C et al.

Background. Virtual reality and interactive digital rehabilitation are marketed for knee osteoarthritis and joint replacement recovery, and the evidence has rarely been sorted by how immersive the technology is.

What they did. This review pooled 21 trials, 12 in knee osteoarthritis and 9 after total knee replacement, grouping interventions by immersion level and clinical mechanism and grading certainty with GRADE.

What they found. For knee osteoarthritis, interactive digital rehabilitation may improve function (SMD 0.59) and pain at six to eight weeks (SMD 0.46), with low certainty. After knee replacement, it may help function with little effect on pain. Fully immersive VR exercise produced inconsistent results, and few trials reported adverse events.

Bottom line. Interactive digital rehabilitation showed promise for knee osteoarthritis care, with low certainty. VR after knee replacement still needs efficacy and safety trials before it earns a place in routine recovery.

8
Digital & hybrid delivery
Patient experiences with digital health interventions in self-management of rheumatic and musculoskeletal diseases: a systematic review and qualitative meta-synthesis.
BMJ Open · Qualitative meta-synthesis · PMID 42778233 · DOI
Truong N et al.

Background. Digital self-management tools for musculoskeletal and rheumatic conditions keep launching, and uptake depends on what patients actually want from them.

What they did. This review synthesized 22 qualitative and mixed-methods studies on patient experiences with asynchronous or hybrid digital programs for rheumatic and musculoskeletal diseases.

What they found. Patients consistently wanted digital tools as an add-on to conventional care, with their clinician still involved. Six themes emerged, led by keeping engagement up (21 of 22 studies), program-related barriers (19 of 22), and empowerment (18 of 22), with repeated requests for clinician involvement.

Bottom line. Patients are asking for hybrid care. Programs that keep a clinician visibly in the loop are more likely to be adopted.

9
Digital & hybrid delivery
Home-based digital exercise versus hospital physiotherapy for plantar fasciitis: a propensity-score-matched retrospective cohort study with dual-perspective cost-utility evaluation.
Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine · Propensity-matched retrospective cohort with cost-utility analysis · PMID 42683610 · DOI
Xu W et al.

Background. Home-based digital exercise programs are being offered as an alternative to hospital-supervised physiotherapy, and this study compared clinical results and costs in routine care at a Chinese tertiary hospital.

What they did. Using hospital records, researchers matched 587 adults with plantar fasciitis treated in 2022 to 2024 who followed either a 12-week home-based digital exercise program or 12 weeks of hospital-supervised physiotherapy, adjusting for 13 baseline characteristics. The main outcome was first-step morning pain at 3 months, and a cost-utility analysis covered 12 months.

What they found. The authors concluded digital exercise was non-inferior for first-step pain at 3 months (adjusted difference -0.63 points on a 0 to 10 scale, 95 percent CI -0.92 to -0.35). It was also associated with lower costs from both perspectives: about US$684 less for the payer and US$974 less for society, with a small gain in quality-adjusted life years (0.011).

Bottom line. Home digital exercise was associated with similar outcomes and lower costs than hospital physiotherapy, but the authors call the findings provisional. Matching left 12 of 13 baseline measures imbalanced and only 6.3 percent of screened patients were eligible, so a randomized trial is needed before reimbursement decisions.

Digital & hybrid delivery
Remote Therapeutic Monitoring Use Among Commercially Insured and Medicare Advantage Patients.
JMIR Rehabilitation and Assistive Technologies · Descriptive national claims analysis · PMID 42766843 · DOI
Joo JH et al.

Background. Remote therapeutic monitoring (RTM) lets clinicians track exercise adherence, function, and symptoms between visits, and its billing codes have existed for only a few years. Studies of how it is actually used remain limited.

What they did. Researchers used 2022 to 2024 MarketScan insurance claims to count RTM use (CPT codes 98975, 98976, 98977, 98980, and 98981) among commercially insured and Medicare Advantage patients, and described who used it, which clinicians delivered it, and for what diagnoses.

What they found. RTM was used by 16,116 patients (12,951 commercially insured and 3,165 Medicare Advantage). Months of RTM use grew from 2,089 in 2022 to 24,494 in 2024, yet use stayed low overall. Use was about four times higher in Medicare Advantage (24.3 versus 5.6 months per 10,000 enrollees). Musculoskeletal conditions were the leading diagnosis in both groups (56.3 percent of commercial and 45.0 percent of Medicare Advantage use). Therapists, nurses, and similar clinicians delivered 48.7 percent of commercial RTM, while primary care physicians delivered 37.8 percent of Medicare Advantage RTM.

Bottom line. RTM is growing quickly from a small base, and musculoskeletal care is its main use. The study is descriptive and leaves out Traditional Medicare, so it cannot say whether monitoring improves outcomes or lowers spending. The delivery patterns here are a useful baseline while CMS weighs changes to RTM rules (see Policy).

11
Access & equity
Understanding consumer preferences for allied health telehealth services in Australia: a discrete choice experiment.
Health Policy · Discrete choice experiment · PMID 42704979 · DOI
Savira F et al.

Background. Telehealth can widen access to physiotherapy, and most preference research comes from medical visits, which may not transfer to hands-on disciplines.

What they did. Researchers ran a discrete choice experiment with 1,001 Australian adults, including 285 rural and 194 culturally and linguistically diverse participants, across physiotherapy and psychology scenarios.

What they found. Respondents preferred in-person or hybrid care overall. Telehealth became more attractive when it was reliable, timely, and cheaper, and technical problems were penalized. Rural respondents were most sensitive to delays and glitches, and culturally diverse respondents were most sensitive to cost. About 47 percent fell into a traditionalist, cost-sensitive group.

Bottom line. Hybrid physiotherapy is the preferred default, and reliability and price decide whether people pick the virtual option.

12
Patient education
Adding the short, digital PainSMART-educational strategy whilst patients with musculoskeletal pain wait for primary care physiotherapy: A randomised controlled trial.
Musculoskeletal Science and Practice · Multicenter randomized controlled trial · PMID 42731318 · DOI
Thompson R et al.

Background. Waiting lists for physiotherapy are long, and the wait could be used to start pain education.

What they did. This Swedish trial randomized 504 adults seeking primary care physiotherapy for musculoskeletal pain to usual care alone or usual care plus PainSMART, a seven-minute pain science film watched before the first visit and briefly discussed with the physiotherapist.

What they found. The study did not show a difference in pain intensity at any time point. Pain self-efficacy was higher for the PainSMART group before the first visit in an exploratory analysis, but that difference faded once physiotherapy began, and fewer PainSMART patients reported getting worse.

Bottom line. The study failed to show added benefit from a seven-minute film once care started. It may still be a cheap way to start improving beliefs and confidence while patients wait.

13
Patient education
Is Exercise Effective at Reducing Pain Catastrophizing in People with Chronic Non-Specific Spinal Pain? A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.
The Clinical Journal of Pain · Systematic review and meta-analysis · PMID 42786912 · DOI
Yu CWG et al.

Background. Pain catastrophizing predicts poor recovery, and exercise is often credited with lowering it alongside pain and disability.

What they did. This review pooled eight trials (1,271 participants) of exercise for chronic nonspecific back or neck pain that measured catastrophizing.

What they found. For low back pain, the primary analysis did not show a significant reduction in catastrophizing at short or intermediate term, with high heterogeneity. Removing one motor control trial produced moderate effects (SMD 0.49 short term, 0.67 intermediate term). Neck strengthening showed low-certainty benefit. All studies had some concerns or high risk of bias.

Bottom line. Exercise may ease catastrophizing, but the evidence is thin. If catastrophizing is the target, pair exercise with interventions built for it.

14
Care models
Interdisciplinary Occupational Health System for Recovery From Musculoskeletal Injuries in US Army Personnel.
JAMA Network Open · Retrospective cohort with IPTW · PMID 42752906 · DOI
Murray TA et al.

Background. Musculoskeletal injuries are the leading cause of lost duty days in the military, and embedding clinicians in the workplace is one proposed fix.

What they did. Using eight years of Army data (1,420,195 musculoskeletal injuries), researchers compared limited-duty time for soldiers in units with an embedded Holistic Health and Fitness team, including on-site physical therapists, athletic trainers, and strength coaches, against soldiers in units without one, with inverse probability weighting.

What they found. Embedded teams were linked to 5.9 percent fewer limited-duty workdays, with larger effects for lower-body injuries. That works out to roughly 3.3 to 4.0 million workdays restored and $93 to $164 million a year in productivity.

Bottom line. Putting MSK clinicians where people work speeds return to duty at scale. The finding supports onsite and near-site models for employers in physically demanding industries.

15
Care models
Effectiveness of care delivery models for knee osteoarthritis in primary healthcare settings: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.
Osteoarthritis and Cartilage · Systematic review and meta-analysis · PMID 42697360 · DOI
Fong HP et al.

Background. Health systems keep redesigning how knee osteoarthritis care is organized in primary care, through care coordination, nurse-led models, and technology, without clear evidence on how much those redesigns help.

What they did. This review pooled 15 randomized trials (2,944 patients) comparing organized care delivery models against usual care in primary care, classified by the Cochrane EPOC taxonomy.

What they found. Care delivery models were associated with improvements in pain (SMD 0.40) and function (SMD 0.26) with moderate certainty, and the analysis showed little to no difference in quality of life. Neither gain reached the prespecified minimal clinically important difference. Models using information and communication technology looked promising in subgroups of only two or three trials.

Bottom line. Reorganizing knee osteoarthritis care may help a little. Technology-supported coordination is the version worth testing in larger pragmatic trials.

16
Care models
Implementing Integrated Whole-Person Care for Chronic Pain: Lessons from VA's Multisite wHOPE Study.
Journal of General Internal Medicine · Qualitative implementation study · PMID 42806223 · DOI
Purcell N et al.

Background. The VA's Whole Health Team, a medical provider, an integrative health provider, and a health coach working together, beat group CBT and usual care for chronic pain in a 764-patient trial. Scaling a team model is a separate problem.

What they did. Researchers interviewed 43 clinicians and staff across six VA medical centers, guided by the PRISM implementation framework, about what helped and hindered the team model.

What they found. Staff valued the model and described what it needs to run: consistent team membership, protected clinician time, clear roles, routine huddles, close ties to primary care, referral pathways to complementary therapies, and skilled administrative coordination.

Bottom line. Staff found whole-person pain teams worthwhile. Coordinator support and protected time look like the line items that decide whether they last.

17
Care models
From research to practice: adapting graded sensorimotor retraining for people with chronic low back pain.
PAIN · Intervention adaptation study · PMID 42751726 · DOI
Venter M et al.

Background. The RESOLVE trial showed graded sensorimotor retraining produced meaningful, lasting benefit for chronic low back pain in a sham-controlled trial. Moving it into everyday clinics is the next step.

What they did. The team ran a two-stage adaptation process: evidence review, stakeholder interviews, and expert consultation to separate core from adaptable components, then a structured consensus meeting to finalize changes.

What they found. The group agreed on eight adaptations covering content, delivery, resources, and clinician training. Most refined or streamlined existing components and did not add or remove whole elements.

Bottom line. RESOLVE now has a practical blueprint for clinical rollout. The adaptation method is reusable for any complex MSK program moving from trial to practice.

18
Health economics
Systematic review of cost-effectiveness analyses of weight loss interventions for knee osteoarthritis.
BMJ Open · Systematic review of economic evaluations · PMID 42772870 · DOI
Gao Y et al.

Background. Weight loss is a core treatment for knee osteoarthritis, and with GLP-1 drugs in the mix, payers need to know which approaches give the best value.

What they did. This review synthesized eight economic evaluations, six models and two trials, of weight-loss interventions for knee osteoarthritis, standardizing costs to 2024 British pounds.

What they found. Diet and exercise programs gained 0.05 to 0.16 QALYs at £16,915 to £31,305 per QALY. Bariatric surgery gained 0.81 to 1.7 QALYs at £4,361 to £19,303 per QALY. Drug therapies cost £32,265 to £35,832 per QALY, and telehealth-only programs had mixed results.

Bottom line. Bariatric surgery appeared most cost-effective for knee osteoarthritis with obesity, mostly on US models. GLP-1 value hinges on price, and no UK-based GLP-1 evaluation exists yet.

19
Health economics
Prevention of sickness absence through early identification and rehabilitation of at-risk patients with musculoskeletal disorders (PREVSAM): a cost-effectiveness analysis comparing team-based rehabilitation in primary care vs treatment as usual.
BMC Health Services Research · Trial-based cost-effectiveness analysis · PMID 42806350 · DOI
Bornhöft L et al.

Background. Screening for high risk of work absence and routing those patients to team-based rehabilitation is a popular primary care strategy, and its economics are rarely tested.

What they did. Alongside the Swedish PREVSAM randomized trial, researchers compared one-year costs and QALYs for screened, at-risk patients with musculoskeletal disorders who received team-based rehabilitation or usual care, from health care and societal perspectives.

What they found. QALYs were similar. Team-based care cost more from the health care perspective, while lower productivity losses roughly offset those costs from the societal perspective. The probability of cost-effectiveness hovered near 50 percent across Swedish thresholds.

Bottom line. The analysis suggested team-based rehab for at-risk workers roughly broke even for society and failed to show a clear economic advantage. Better screening to find who needs the team is the lever.

20
Measurement & prognosis
Physical, Psychophysical and Psychological Predictors of Persistent or Recurrent Non-Specific Low Back Pain: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
European Journal of Pain · Systematic review and meta-analysis · PMID 42695120 · DOI
Wongwitwichote K et al.

Background. Knowing which back pain patients will go on to persistent pain would let clinicians focus early effort, and many candidate predictors have been proposed.

What they did. This review pooled 15 prospective studies testing physical, psychophysical, and psychological predictors of persistent or recurrent nonspecific low back pain.

What they found. Depression and distress (15,778 participants) and fear avoidance (1,105 participants) predicted persistent pain and disability, both at low certainty. Pain sensitization measured by temporal summation did not predict outcomes, and evidence on trunk movement predictors was limited.

Bottom line. Distress and fear avoidance carried the most predictive signal so far, at low certainty, and both are modifiable, so early screening is reasonable.

Measurement & prognosis
Unsupervised identification of population clusters with distinct low back pain profiles.
The Spine Journal · Cross-sectional machine learning analysis · PMID 42679904 · DOI
Fernández Schlein FA et al.

Background. Population programs for back pain usually treat everyone alike, and data-driven clustering may reveal groups that need different approaches.

What they did. Researchers applied a clustering algorithm to 6,233 respondents in Chile's nationally representative 2016 to 2017 health survey, using sociodemographic, psychosocial, and biomarker data.

What they found. Three groups emerged. Middle-aged adults with heavier depressive symptoms had the highest back pain prevalence (27 percent) and the most intense pain. Socioeconomically disadvantaged older adults with multimorbidity had 24 percent prevalence, the longest pain duration, and the most pain sites. Healthy young adults had 11 percent.

Bottom line. Back pain at the population level splits along mood and cardiometabolic lines. Population programs may work better when they target those groups separately.

Measurement & prognosis
Successful Blinding or Wishful Thinking? A Secondary Analysis of a Randomised Controlled Trial of Manual Therapy.
European Journal of Pain · Secondary analysis of an RCT · PMID 42765154 · DOI
Gevers-Montoro C et al.

Background. Placebo-controlled trials of spinal manipulation depend on participants not knowing which treatment they got, and blinding is rarely measured.

What they did. Using a placebo-controlled trial of spinal manipulation for chronic primary low back pain (98 participants), researchers measured blinding with the Bang index at three time points and compared outcomes between blinded and unblinded participants.

What they found. Blinding was adequate at the study level: correct guesses in the active group were balanced by wishful thinking in the placebo group. Treatment effects were markedly larger in participants who believed they knew their assignment, and adverse events did not differ.

Bottom line. Belief about treatment appears to shape manual therapy outcomes. Trials should measure and report blinding as routinely as they report pain.

Devices & technology
Early national comparison of robotic versus conventional knee replacements for arthritis using National Joint Registry data: target trial emulation study.
BMJ · Target trial emulation · PMID 42815981 · DOI
Mohammad HR et al.

Background. Robotic-assisted knee replacement is spreading fast despite higher capital and per-case costs, and large-scale evidence on whether implants last longer has been missing.

What they did. Using the UK National Joint Registry, researchers emulated a target trial across 697,145 knee replacements from 2018 to 2024, including 22,111 robotic procedures, with propensity score matching.

What they found. Five-year implant survival was nearly identical for total knee replacement (98.6 percent robotic versus 98.5 percent conventional) and the study did not show a difference for partial knee replacement. The study did not show differences in revision causes or intraoperative complications.

Bottom line. Early national data failed to show an implant survival advantage for robotic knee replacement. Systems should weigh the extra cost carefully until longer follow-up or patient-reported benefits appear.

Devices & technology
U.S. Veterans with low back pain benefit from transcranial direct current stimulation: Results from a cognitive behavioral therapy randomized controlled trial.
The Journal of Pain · Double-blind sham-controlled RCT · PMID 42700878 · DOI
Douglass MA et al.

Background. Cognitive behavioral therapy for chronic pain is the VA's recommended nonpharmacologic treatment, and many veterans see only modest gains from it.

What they did. This double-blind trial randomized 115 veterans with chronic low back pain and prescription opioid misuse to real or sham transcranial direct current stimulation delivered during 11 CBT sessions.

What they found. The real stimulation group showed a steeper decline in pain and functional impairment over time, though end-of-treatment scores were similar between groups. Opioid misuse fell in both groups and the study did not show added benefit from stimulation.

Bottom line. Brain stimulation may speed progress during CBT for back pain without changing where patients end up. Whether faster gains justify the added equipment and visit time remains open.

Devices & technology
Readiness for artificial intelligence adoption among rehabilitation professionals: an international cross-sectional survey.
Disability and Rehabilitation · International cross-sectional survey · PMID 42721383 · DOI
Cunningham S et al.

Background. Artificial intelligence tools are moving into rehabilitation practice, and it matters whether the clinicians expected to use them are prepared to do so safely and ethically.

What they did. Researchers surveyed rehabilitation professionals recruited through an online education platform, using an AI readiness scale and a technology acceptance model, then looked at what was linked to their intention to use AI.

What they found. A total of 2,496 people responded, from diverse regions. Intention to use AI was relatively high, but overall readiness was only moderate. Intention was linked to readiness, expected performance benefits, attitude toward AI, self-efficacy, anxiety, age, prior AI use, and profession.

Bottom line. Interest in AI is running ahead of preparation. The authors call for practical training, ethical guidance, and implementation support. The sample came from an online education platform, so it may not represent all clinicians.
